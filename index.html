Why heat stress costs you money

When the shed gets hot, dairy animals eat less, produce less milk, and take longer to get pregnant. Good airflow is one of the cheapest ways to protect your herd and your income.

  • 🥛Lower milk yield when animals are heat stressed
  • 🌾Reduced feed intake during hot afternoons
  • 🐄Fertility and health problems through the summer
  • 💨Stale air, ammonia and flies inside closed sheds

High-capacity box exhaust fans pull hot, stale air and ammonia out of the shed and draw fresh air in. Ideal for closed and tunnel-ventilated sheds.

HVLS Ceiling Fans

High-Volume Low-Speed giant ceiling fans move a huge volume of air gently across open sheds. One fan can replace many small fans and use less energy.

FAQ

Common questions

Which fan is best for my dairy farm?

It depends on your shed. Open sheds with high roofs often do best with HVLS fans. Closed sheds need exhaust fans for tunnel ventilation. Most farms use wall-mounted circulation fans over the stalls and feeding lane, with mist fans added for very hot areas. We recommend the right mix after a site survey.

Can dairy fans run on solar power?

Yes. Solar works very well for dairy fans because fans run hardest when the sun is strongest. The system must be sized to the real fan load. We design the panels, inverter and wiring together with the fans.

How far apart should fans be installed?

A common rule is to place circulation fans about 10 times their diameter apart in a row. For example, 36-inch fans go about every 30 feet and 50-inch fans about every 40 feet. Fans should also be tilted slightly downward to blow onto the animals. Use our fan calculator for a quick estimate.

Do mist fans make the shed wet?

A properly designed mist system uses very fine droplets that evaporate in the air before they reach the floor. Mist works best in dry heat and needs good airflow to carry moisture away.
